# v2: feature groups, engine work and decisions

Date: 2026-10-17 · Owner: ibaranov91 · Status: planning. Built on the local `v2` branch, released after the hackathon
(see "Now: building v2" in AGENTS.md).

Everything below comes from one list of ideas, grouped, then refined by the decisions made on 2026-10-17
("Decisions" at the end records each one). Items marked **open** still need a call before they're built.

## Build order
Foundations first: they make the later groups cheaper and keep CPU low as animated things arrive.

| Phase | Groups | Why this order |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Engine improvements (render on demand, build info, logging, notification center + journal, items/containers/props) | Everything else plugs into these |
| 1 | Voice and audio, presence and interruptions, reliability | Fixes people feel every day |
| 2 | Mac client, meetings and calendar | Depend on DND, the notification center and build info |
| 3 | Items, inventory, storage | Needs the item registry and containers |
| 4 | Map, places, objects (7/11, kitchen, Book Club) | Needs items; the 7/11 sells them |
| 5 | Atmosphere (night, weather, fog, windows, roof) | Needs render on demand and the lighting pass |
| 6 | Social and expression (say bubble, photos, shared reading) | Needs per-person file storage and the notification slots |
| 7 | NPCs | Needs NPC routines; the ghost and cashier reuse them |
| 8 | Terminal client | Optional; last |

## Feature groups

### 1. Voice and audio
- **Deafen means silence.** While deafened, entering a jukebox room starts no audio at all: don't create or play the
  `<audio>` element, since starting playback is what makes macOS switch to AirPods.
- **Jukebox starts quiet.** Entering a room with a jukebox plays it at 5–10%; the volume slider sits in the top area
  (see "Pop-up areas").
- **More than 12 in an open-voice room: stage + waiting line** (decision 7). Keep `ROOM_VOICE_MAX` = 12 speakers and
  polish today's waiting line: a clear "On stage / Waiting (3rd)" state, a way to step down and give your place, and
  the next person promoted automatically. Listeners past 12 still hear the room through the mesh, so every speaker
  uploads to everyone in the room: in a room of ~30 that is heavy on speakers' upload and CPU. Watch it; an SFU is the
  fallback if real use hits it.
- **Push to talk:** choose your own key; when PTT is on, say so plainly in the sidebar's bottom strip and in the voice and
  call pop-ups.

### 2. Presence, interruptions and notifications
- **Deafen ≠ DND.** Deafen only turns sound off: knocks still pop up.
- **DND:** a moon in the top bar (like iOS) and in the Mac app's right-click menu. While on, your bubble shows the moon;
  people can still knock, but knocks go straight to your knock queue without a pop-up.
- **Knock queue:** at most the last 2 knocks as pop-ups; older ones move to the activity log.
- **Activity log:** knocks from you and to you, calls, meeting and private-room history, and later thefts, gifts,
  photos and achievements. It reads from the event journal (engine item 9).
- **Pop-up areas** (decision 4):
  - **Top = what is happening to you now:** call and voice status, incoming knocks (max 2), the meeting heads-up,
    jukebox volume.
  - **Bottom = a mode you are in, with its exit button:** End ride, presenting, sewing, held-item actions.
  - **One-off notices** ("You got a hat", "Kim took your mug") float briefly over your own character, game-style, then
    live in the activity log.
- **Say bubble** (decision 5): press Enter (or tap a bubble button on phones) for a one-line input right above your
  character; Enter posts it and the bubble lasts ~10 s. Anyone whose screen shows your character sees it.

### 3. Meetings and calendar
- **Mac meeting reminder:** extend the existing native heads-up card under the menu bar icon with **Open Meet**,
  **Go to room** and **Both**, plus a setting to turn meeting reminders off.
- **Simpler meetings** (proposed, **open**): you pick a default once ("I join meetings in Meet" or "in The Deck"); the
  heads-up then shows only **Join** and **Later**, with the other way under "…". Joining Meet turns in-game voice off and
  shows you as "On Meet" in the room, so there's never a choice between two voices.
- **Calendar hover:** hovering a wall calendar shows your next meeting. It reads the `agenda` the server already sends
  every 2 min, so the client needs no extra syncing.

### 4. Mac client
- **Always on top:** a pin mode that keeps the window above every other window. Drop "move desktop window": the window can
  always be moved and resized, and grabbed by a strip along its top edge.
- **Client version** in the right-click menu (and the server version, see group 5).
- **DND** in the right-click menu, next to Mute microphone and Disable sound.
- **Settings window** (proposed, **open**): a small native window (⌘,) for OS-level things: launch at login, instance,
  pin mode, notifications, meeting reminders on/off. Game settings (PTT key, audio) stay in the web page so they work in
  browsers too; the right-click menu keeps quick toggles only.
- **Testing releases locally:** dev builds get their own bundle id (`io.t1a.deck.dev`, own name and icon) so they run
  next to the installed app; the Instance menu gets "Local (localhost:5173)"; the dev server serves a `--dist` build at
  `/api/mac` so the self-update path can be tested; a short release checklist (install, update, mic permission kept,
  knock card, meeting card, pin modes, sleep/wake).

### 5. Reliability, performance, diagnostics
- **Idle CPU** (25% of one M4 core today): render on demand (engine item 11), lower FPS when nothing moves or the window
  is in the background, fewer server updates for an idle client.
- **Losing the connection for a moment:** the server keeps your place and call seat for 30–60 s with a resume token, so a
  quick reconnect doesn't drop you or walk you off. The web page shows a dimmed "Reconnecting…" bar, retries with
  backoff and reconnects voice itself. The Mac app reconnects at once on wake and network change, and greys the menu bar
  person while offline.
- **Server version** shown in the UI (e.g. the sidebar's bottom strip or the about line), from engine item 13.
- **Logging** (decision 6), all four pieces:
  - **Client error reports:** uncaught errors and failed promises from browsers and the Mac app go to a per-day file on
    the instance, like voice diagnostics, kept 3 days.
  - **Report bug button** on the suggestions board: a short note plus the last ~200 events from your session, your
    versions and a screenshot.
  - **Server event log:** short structured lines (connect, disconnect, rejected intent with its reason, crash) in the
    system journal.
  - **Admin log viewer:** a GM-panel page with the latest errors and reports, so nobody needs ssh.

### 6. Atmosphere
- **Cozier night:** outside gets darker; rooms with no lights are very dark; rooms with lights are lighter depending on
  how many, never bright. Glow sticks (group 8) are small moving lights.
- **Time zones** (proposed, **open**): lighting follows each viewer's local time, while shared events (the ghost's coffee
  breaks) follow one office time zone. The alternatives were one office time for everyone (one world, but night at the
  wrong hour for some) or each viewer's local time for everything (shared events don't line up).
- **Weather outdoors:** rain brings more waves and a black flag on the beach; fog limits how far you see, fading from
  ~5 tiles out. People walking outside in the rain open an umbrella if they have one (the 7/11 sells them) and pull up a
  hood otherwise (**open**: or everyone gets an umbrella automatically, as first planned). An umbrella stays up while
  they eat outside.
- **Windows** open and close; an open window shows rain when it's raining.
- **Berghain roof:** covers the club when you're not inside, so it doesn't distract the Book Club.

### 7. Map, places, objects
- **Layout:** move Berghain to the right of the Admin room, and put a small 7/11 under both.
- **7/11:** sliding doors, its own neon sign, a TV in the corner, shelves (group 8) and a cashier bot (group 10).
  Everything is free with a daily limit per person (decision 1; the number is **open**).
- **Kitchen:** food can be eaten at the kitchen tables too, standing is fine; maybe one bigger bar table. No table voice
  there, since the kitchen is already public talk. A **kettle** for cup noodles.
- **Book Club:** turn the chairs.
- **Mirror:** clicking it does what "Edit look" in the sidebar does.
- **Trophy case:** a glass case with trophies in Co-working as the physical achievement board.
- **Suggestions board:** the bug terminal, renamed, moved outside the Admin room; home of the Report bug button.
- **Pomodoro** (decision 8): no object. A personal timer in your bottom strip with a 🍅 over your sprite while it runs;
  the table object goes away.
- **Terminal room** for the terminal client (group 11).

### 8. Items, inventory, storage
- **Held items:** an item comes out of your pocket into your hands before it can be used, given or thrown.
- **Giving:** take it out, then throw it at someone (it lands in their pocket). Admins can give anyone any item.
- **Pocket limit:** a fixed number of items, hats unlimited (number **open**). Extras go to personal storage.
- **Personal storage:** your desk plant. You can open someone else's, with a warning that you may be spotted; being
  spotted (anyone else in the room) earns a bad achievement. Taking their things gets you a prison hat for 2 days that
  only an admin can remove, and the owner sees who took what.
- **Daily random items** (a few a day around the office):
  - **Snowball:** throw it at someone; it lands in their pocket and both get a snow aura for 10 min.
  - **Choo-choo train:** put it on the floor; everyone passing next to it does the choo-choo gesture.
  - **Remote car:** you drive it through the office while your character stands with a controller; the bottom area holds
    End ride.
  - **Sewing kit:** sit and sew; an hour without stopping makes a hand-sewn hat you can give away.
- **7/11 shelves** (decision 1):
  - Food and drinks: **onigiri** (snack), **cup noodles** (fill at the kitchen kettle, eat at a table), **slurpee** (brain
    freeze: a short shiver and a ❄ over you, stacks with chilled), **energy drink** (bigger speed boost than coffee for
    5 min, then a short slow crash).
  - Fun: **gacha capsule** (a machine by the door; one per person per day; contents **open**), **party popper** (confetti
    over everyone within 2 tiles, with a sound), **glow stick** (a small light around you at night and in Berghain).
  - Useful: **umbrella** (rain, group 6), **postcard** (write a short line, give it; it stays in their pockets),
    **bubble gum** (blow a pink bubble over your head).
- **Achievement hats** (decision 3): at 5, 10 and 15 achievements you pick any hat you don't own yet from the pool:
  shark, cat ears, fox ears, frog, onigiri, apple, coffee cup, mushroom.

### 9. Social and expression
- **Photos:** an always-visible camera button (tested on phones). Photos go to your own folder, 10 MB per person, with
  download and delete, and can be submitted for photo of the week (how the week's photo is picked is **open**).
- **No photos in Berghain:** the bouncer stays put, says a line, and you land outside.
- **Shared reading in Book Club:** a leader opens a book and turns pages; everyone in the room sees it on half the
  screen. Open-source books on the shelves; the leader's chair with a desk is where you pick one.

### 10. NPCs
- **Cleaning lady** waters plants, trees and bushes now and then (nothing dries out yet).
- **Ghost of Mazurkevich** has 4–5 coffee breaks a day, 10 min each at the kitchen table. While there he can be greeted:
  an achievement and a ghost hat.
- **Bouncer:** a new line for the photo kick-out.
- **7/11 cashier** (decision 2), inspired by *Convenience Store Woman*: polite, literal, devoted to the store and its
  manual, carefully copying how normal people act. Name **open**. Approved lines:
  - Greeting:
    - "Irasshaimase! Welcome! I said it at the correct volume. Thank you."
    - "The store heard the door. The store is happy. I am part of the store, so I am also happy."
    - "Good morning. I practised this smile in the freezer door reflection."
    - "Hello. I am behaving like a normal person today. Please tell me if I stop."
  - Checkout:
    - "Would you like this warmed up? I ask everyone. Even about the umbrella."
    - "That is your last one today. The manual says so. I love the manual."
    - "Do you need a bag? You do not have hands for a bag. I will imagine the bag for you."
    - "Onigiri and a slurpee. Yesterday too. You are a very regular customer. That is good." (names what you took)
  - Idle:
    - "If you listen, the shelves tell you what is missing. The onigiri are lonely."
    - "I have worked here eighteen years. The store opened this morning. Both are true."
    - "I speak like the last customer. Today I am a little bit like you."
    - "My family asked when I will be cured. I said: after my shift."
  - When you talk to her or linger:
    - "You are not buying anything. I do not know what you are. Please buy something so I know."
    - "People ask if I will marry. I am married to the 7/11. We are very happy. Mostly."
    - "That is not in the manual. Irasshaimase!"
    - "Outside the store I am not sure which way to face. In here, I face you."

### 11. Terminal client
Feasible: a small Node TUI, or an SSH server (`ssh deck`, nothing to install) speaking the same WebSocket protocol.
Chat and arrow-key walking in a "Terminal" room on the map; the server keeps terminal users inside it, and web clients
draw them as green-phosphor sprites. No voice. Login is the hard part (**open**): SSO relies on the NetBird proxy's
header, so the terminal needs a device-code-style flow (open a link, approve in the browser) or a personal token from
the web UI.

## Engine improvements
Goal: a new item, hat, NPC or panel is one data entry plus art, not wiring across many files.

1. **Item registry** (`shared/items.ts`): one `ItemDef` table for drinks, snacks, hats, drugs and every new item: id,
   kind (hat, held, placeable, consumable, throwable), whether it counts toward the pocket limit, a per-day limit (the
   7/11), its sprite, allowed actions and `onUse` / `onHit` effects. The action wheel builds itself from it; `DRINKS`
   folds into it.
2. **Containers:** pockets, desk storage, 7/11 shelves and the trophy case are containers with an owner. Every item
   move is one server operation, `move(item, from, to, by)`, which enforces limits, prevents duplicates and writes the
   journal (thefts, gifts).
3. **World props:** one saved layer for things placed on floors and tables, each with an owner and an optional time
   limit. Cups already work like this inside `Creatures`; the train, the car and spawned items reuse it, and the cleaner
   can tidy any of them.
4. **Daily spawns:** spawn tiles in map data; the date seeds which items appear where. A new spawnable is one line.
5. **NPC routines as data:** schedule (time windows, in office time), steps ("walk to X, hold pose Y for N min"), lines in
   `shared/phrases.ts`, and an `onInteract` reward. Watering, the ghost's coffee and the cashier are routines.
6. **Rewards table:** milestones and specific achievements map to rewards. A reward can be a fixed item or a choice
   from a pool (achievement hats are "pick one").
7. **Accessory layers:** hat, held item, aura and pose as standard sprite layers, so a new hat or held item is one entry
   in `gen-assets.mjs`.
8. **Notification center:** one client queue with named slots (top, bottom, over-character), priorities and lifetimes.
   Whatever leaves a slot goes to the activity log.
9. **Event journal:** a capped list of events per person on the server; the activity log, "who took my stuff", photo of
   the week and bug reports read from it.
10. **Per-person file storage:** banners, decks and photos share one module for quota, allowed types, validation and
    serving.
11. **Render on demand:** the engine draws only when something changed; animated things declare that they animate.
    Fixes idle CPU and keeps it low as weather, NPCs and toys arrive.
12. **Lighting and weather pass:** lights come from map data (plus moving lights like glow sticks) into a cached
    offscreen lightmap; global darkness comes from time of day and weather; the server broadcasts the weather.
13. **Build info:** one source for server version, web build and Mac build, shown in the UI and attached to bug reports.

## Weight, bugs, CPU (high level)

| Area | CPU / runtime | Bug risk | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rain particles | Medium–high if naive | Low | Visible outdoor tiles only, capped, paused when idle or hidden |
| Fog | Medium | Medium | Cheap as one cached gradient overlay; costly if per tile per frame |
| Night lighting | Low cached, high if per frame | Low | Rebuild the lightmap only when lights or time change |
| More NPCs and routines | Low (server pathfinding) | Medium | Timers and edge cases, as with the ghost and cleaner today |
| Remote car | Medium (a stream of moves) | High | Real-time control fits "server sends a path" badly |
| Voice past 12 (stage + waiting line) | High for speakers in big rooms | Medium | Speakers upload to every listener; SFU is the fallback |
| DND, knock queue, PTT, deafen fix | Low | Medium–high | Touches voice and knock flows that have bitten before |
| Inventory, storage, theft | Low | Medium–high | Duplicates, persistence, abuse; containers (engine 2) contain it |
| Photos, shared reading | Low runtime, more disk | Medium | Reuse presentation and file-storage code |
| Activity log, journal | Low | Low | Cap it |
| Mac always on top, settings | None | Medium | Window-level quirks with Spaces and full screen |
| Terminal client | None in the game | Low, ongoing upkeep | Freezes the protocol more |
| Objects, hats, roof, chairs, mirror | Negligible | Low | — |

Most of v2 is cheap data and state. CPU comes from weather, fog and lighting; bugs from voice and inventory. Render on
demand first means atmosphere starts from a low baseline.

## Answers to the questions in the list
- **How the Google integration works:** per person. The app is one OAuth client (ID and secret) but has no access of its
  own: no service account, no domain-wide delegation. Each person grants access on their own sign-in; the server keeps
  that person's refresh token encrypted in `state.json` and calls Meet and Calendar only on their behalf. Meetings come
  from the server reading every connected calendar every 2 min and sending each person their `agenda`.
- **Testing Mac releases locally, losing the connection, a Mac settings window, simpler meetings, time zones, the
  terminal client:** see groups 4, 5, 4, 3, 6 and 11.
